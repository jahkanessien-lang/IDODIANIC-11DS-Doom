11-DIMENSIONAL SECURITY

Security designed to survive failure.

11DS is a defense-in-depth security architecture for protecting an entire technology ecosystem—from identity and data to AI, infrastructure, physical systems, resilience, and independent assurance.

Explore 11DSRead the documentation

11DS does not claim literal “unbreakability.” Its goal is controlled survivability: prevent, detect, contain, preserve evidence, recover cleanly, and continuously improve.

One architecture. Eleven dimensions.

11DS treats security as a system of mutually reinforcing controls rather than a single perimeter. A failure in one layer should not automatically become total compromise.

The 11 dimensions

01 — People, Identity & Trust

Authentication, authorization, privileged access, machine identity, zero trust and human factors.

02 — Data & Cryptography

Encryption, key management, integrity, signatures, classification, privacy and post-quantum readiness.

03 — Devices & Platforms

Secure boot, firmware, operating systems, endpoint security, device posture and platform integrity.

04 — Applications & Supply Chain

Secure development, dependencies, SBOMs, provenance, signed releases and software assurance.

05 — Infrastructure, Cloud & Compute

Servers, cloud, containers, virtualization, storage, infrastructure-as-code and control-plane protection.

06 — Networks & Communications

Segmentation, zero-trust networking, secure remote access, communications resilience and lateral-movement resistance.

07 — Operations, Detection & Response

SOC, SIEM, threat hunting, incident response, evidence preservation and defensive automation.

08 — Physical & Cyber-Physical Security

Facilities, power, cooling, physical access, OT and cyber-physical safety boundaries.

09 — AI, Agents, Quantum & Emerging Tech

AI permissions, model security, prompt-injection resistance, agent governance and PQC migration.

10 — Risk, Resilience & Continuity

Immutable backups, disaster recovery, clean-room recovery, alternate sites and continuity planning.

11 — Governance & Independent Assurance

Policies, ownership, audits, suppliers, compliance, independent testing and continuous improvement.

Design principle

Assume components can fail. Build the architecture so one stolen credential, compromised device, malicious dependency, rogue agent, cloud failure or operational mistake does not become an unrestricted path to everything else.

Security objective

  • Reduce attack surface
  • Limit blast radius
  • Detect abnormal behavior
  • Contain safely
  • Preserve trustworthy evidence
  • Recover from clean state
  • Independently verify controls
